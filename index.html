Body OS · Reference Guide
Your body manual
📌 Reference cards — read them whenever you need a reset, not just once. The goal is to internalize until automatic.
🪑
Sitting posture
The position doing the most long-term damage if unchecked.
Sit on your sit bones, not your tailbone. Scoot forward — feel two bony points under each glute, not your lower back rounding into the backrest.Most people slump onto their sacrum all day.
Screen at eye level. Looking down at a laptop for hours compresses the cervical spine. Prop your screen or use an external monitor.
Shoulders down, not braced. Don't hold them — let them drop. The yin and band work are training this to happen naturally.
No position is correct for 8 hours. Shift, fidget, stand. Position variety is the goal — not rigid perfect posture.
🧍
Standing posture
Standing is better than sitting — but bad standing still causes pain.
Weight even across both feet. Most people load one hip chronically. Stand hip-width, feel equal pressure in both heels and balls of feet.
Soft bend in knees. Locked knees compress joints and shift the pelvis forward. An almost imperceptible flex protects both.
Neutral pelvis. Swimmers often have anterior pelvic tilt (butt sticking out). Lightly engage your lower abs. Dragon pose is fixing this structurally over time.
🛋️
Couch & bed working
You're going to do it — here's how to make it less damaging.
Back support is non-negotiable. A firm pillow or rolled towel at the lumbar. Without it, the spine collapses within minutes on a soft couch.
Prop your screen up. Laptop in your lap = neck at 45°. Use a lap desk or pillow stack to get it closer to eye level.
Stand break every 45–60 min. Set a timer. Two minutes standing resets circulation and spinal loading. You'll come back more focused.
🛏️
Sleep positions
7 hours of bad position undoes a lot of good work during the day.
Best: side with pillow between knees. Keeps hips stacked, prevents spinal rotation. Pillow should fill the full ear-to-shoulder gap. Painful shoulder faces UP.Most people use a pillow that's too flat.
Also good: back with pillow under knees. Decompresses lumbar and releases hip flexors. Use a thinner pillow for your head. Shoulders will externally rotate — that's correct.
Avoid: stomach sleeping. Forces hours of neck rotation, compresses lumbar, locks shoulders in internal rotation — reinforcing every pattern you're trying to undo.
🦷
Jaw resting state & bruxism
You already have the resting position right. The work is training the system that drives the clenching.
Correct resting position (N position): Lips together, teeth slightly apart (1–2mm), tongue resting gently on the palate. You do this naturally — reinforce it consciously until it's fully automatic.
Daytime clenching trigger: Stress and concentration. Do a jaw check every time you pick up your phone. Teeth should not be touching unless you're eating or speaking.
The breathing practice IS the treatment. Box breathing and extended exhale lower the cortisol that drives grinding. Four to six weeks of daily consistency will show measurable change. The jaw exercises manage the symptom — the breathwork addresses the cause.
🍬
Oral fixation — healthy redirects
The urge is real. The goal is to redirect it toward things that don't worsen your jaw or teeth.
Sipping water is your best option. Satisfies the oral urge, counts toward your hydration goal, and gives your hands something to do. Keep a bottle visible at all times.
Herbal tea. Warm liquid is naturally calming, satisfies the oral fixation, and adds to hydration. Chamomile or peppermint before bed also supports sleep.
Sugar-free mints. Low jaw engagement compared to gum — satisfies the mouth sensation without heavily loading the masseter.
Crunchy vegetables — occasional, not all day. Carrots, celery, cucumber. Fine as a snack but not a constant habit. Prolonged chewing tires the same muscles already overworked by bruxism.
Avoid: chewing gum regularly. Feels harmless but it's exactly the repetitive masseter loading that worsens bruxism. Occasional use is fine — daily gum chewing is counterproductive to everything your jaw exercises are trying to achieve.
The deeper fix: Oral fixation is often a displaced stress response — the same nervous system state driving the clenching. When you notice the urge strongly, that's a cue to do one round of box breathing before reaching for anything.
💧
Hydration — especially in Denver
At 5,280 ft with low humidity, dehydration is faster and sneakier than at sea level.
Why altitude is different: You breathe faster to compensate for lower oxygen. Each exhale carries more water vapor. You can lose an extra liter+ per day through breathing alone — without sweating at all.
Denver target: 12 cups (96 oz / ~3L) minimum. On workout days, add 2 more cups per hour of exercise. Sea level standard (8 cups) is not enough in Denver.
Drink on a schedule, not when thirsty. Thirst lags dehydration by 1–2% body weight — by the time you feel it, you're already impaired. Morning glass before coffee, then a glass with every meal and habit block.
Add electrolytes on workout days. Plain water without minerals is less effective at altitude. A pinch of sea salt in your water bottle or an electrolyte tablet does the job without sugary sports drinks.
Coffee and alcohol are more dehydrating at altitude. Both are diuretics — their effect is amplified at elevation. For every coffee, drink an equal amount of water alongside it. This also helps with the cortisol spike.
