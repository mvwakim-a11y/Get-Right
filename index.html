Body manual
Reference cards — internalize until automatic
Sitting posture
1 of 8
Sitting posture
The position you're in most — and the one doing the most damage unchecked.
The goal isn't rigid perfect posture for 8 hours. It's frequent small resets and position variety throughout the day.
form
Do this
Sit on your sit bones, not your tailbone
Scoot forward slightly — feel two bony points under each glute. The moment you slump back, your lumbar loads and your shoulders round forward.
screen
Do this
Screen at eye level
Looking down at a laptop adds ~10 lbs of load per inch of forward head. Prop your screen, use a stand, or get an external monitor.
posture
Do this
Drop your shoulders, don't brace them
Actively releasing the brace trains the nervous system. The band work and yin are making this the natural default — for now, just notice and release.
variety
Key insight
No position is correct for 8 hours
Shift, fidget, stand. Research is clear — sustained static posture causes more harm than moderate movement. Variety is the goal.
Standing posture
Better than sitting — but only if the mechanics are right.
balance
Do this
Weight even across both feet
Most people load one hip chronically. Stand hip-width apart, equal pressure in heels and balls of both feet. This alone fixes a lot.
knees
Do this
Soft knee bend — never locked
Locked knees compress the joint and push the pelvis forward. An almost imperceptible flex changes the whole chain above it.
swim
Swimmer note
Watch for anterior pelvic tilt
Butt sticking out, lower back arched. Common in swimmers from hip flexor tightness. Lightly engage lower abs. Dragon pose is correcting this structurally — the standing cue reinforces it.
target
Target shape
Ribs stacked over hips, chest open
Imagine breathing into your back as well as your front — this naturally lifts the ribcage without flaring it forward. Ears over shoulders, shoulders over hips, hips over ankles.
Couch & bed working
You're going to do it — here's how to make it significantly less damaging.
support
Non-negotiable
Firm lumbar support, always
A rolled towel or firm pillow at the lower back. Without it, a soft couch collapses your spine within 5 minutes. This single addition changes everything about couch working.
angle
Do this
Prop your screen toward eye level
Laptop in lap = 45° neck angle for hours. A lap desk, pillow stack, or even a shoebox gets you closer. Imperfect is still dramatically better than nothing.
timer
Habit trigger
Stand break every 45–60 min
Set a timer. Two minutes upright resets circulation and spinal loading. Your concentration will also improve — brief movement breaks measurably increase focus.
avoid
Avoid
Extended side-lying work
20 min here or there is fine. Extended sessions cause uneven neck loading and shoulder compression on the bottom arm. If you're watching something, fine — if you're working, find a better position.
Sleep positions
7 hours of bad position undoes a lot of good daily work.
Your shoulder pain almost certainly comes from position — either sleeping on the painful side, or a pillow that's too flat for side sleeping.
✓ Best
Side sleeping
Pillow between knees. Pillow fills full ear-to-shoulder gap. Painful shoulder faces up.
↗ Also good
Back sleeping
Pillow under knees. Thinner head pillow. Shoulders externally rotate — that's correct, not wrong.
Avoid
Stomach sleeping
Forces hours of neck rotation, compresses lumbar, locks shoulders in internal rotation — directly reinforcing every pattern you're trying to undo.
pillow
Pillow tip
Your pillow is probably too flat
Side sleepers need a firm, tall pillow that fills the full head-to-shoulder gap. If your neck is bent downward during sleep you'll wake up in pain regardless of position.
Jaw resting state
You already have the position right. The work is training the system that drives clenching.
Tongue on roof of mouth is correct whether awake or asleep. Grinding during sleep is NOT caused by jaw position — it's driven by nervous system arousal during sleep microarousals.
rest
N position
Lips together · teeth slightly apart · tongue to palate
You already do this naturally. The goal is making it fully automatic — especially during concentration, screen time, and driving.
phone
Habit anchor
Jaw check every time you pick up your phone
Teeth should not be touching unless you're eating or speaking. 50 micro-checks a day builds the awareness that stops daytime clenching.
breathe
Root cause
The breathwork IS the treatment
Box breathing and extended exhale lower the cortisol and autonomic arousal that trigger sleep bruxism. 4–6 weeks of daily consistency shows measurable change. Jaw exercises manage the symptom — breathwork addresses the cause.
Oral fixation — redirects
The urge is real. Redirect toward things that don't worsen your jaw.
Oral fixation is often a displaced stress response — the same state driving the clenching. When the urge is strong, that's a signal to breathe first.

Hydration in Denver
At 5,280 ft with low humidity, dehydration is faster and sneakier than at sea level.
You lose water with every single exhale. At altitude, you breathe faster and each breath carries more vapor. Drink on schedule — thirst lags dehydration.

salt
Workout days
Add electrolytes, not just water
Plain water at altitude is less effective without minerals. A pinch of sea salt in your bottle or an electrolyte tablet — not sugary sports drinks.
coffee
Watch this
Coffee and alcohol hit harder at altitude
Both are diuretics with amplified effect at elevation. For every coffee, drink an equal amount of water alongside it. Alcohol before bed also measurably increases grinding episodes.
Stopping the grind
Sleep bruxism is centrally mediated — your nervous system during microarousals. You can't consciously stop it, but you can reduce how often and how hard it fires.
Everything in this plan — breathwork, nasal training, morning sunlight, yin yoga — directly addresses the upstream causes of bruxism. It's the mechanism, not a side benefit.
why
What it is
A neurological event, not a positional one
Caused by microarousals triggering ANS activation. Jaw position, tongue, occlusion — none of these cause it. Research has entirely moved away from peripheral/dental explanations.
Thomas et al., JADA 2024
rct
RCT Evidence
Respiratory training reduces grinding
12 weeks of respiratory muscle training significantly reduced sleep bruxism. Your breathwork plan is doing exactly this.
NIH-indexed RCT, 2024
airway
Airway link
Nasal breathing training is directly therapeutic
When the airway collapses during sleep, the jaw reflexively clenches to reopen it. Nasal training addresses this root cause — not just altitude.
supp
Worth trying
Magnesium glycinate before bed — 300–400mg
~75% of Americans are below the RDA. Deficiency → muscle hyperactivity + elevated cortisol + poor sleep. Ask your doctor first.
Ploceniak 1990; Boyle et al. 2017
avoid
High leverage
Alcohol before bed measurably increases grinding
Disrupts sleep architecture, increases microarousals, consistently increases bruxism frequency. One of the highest-impact behavioral changes available.
guard
Insurance
Wear your night guard every night
Doesn't stop grinding but prevents tooth damage. Behavioral and breathwork changes take 4–8 weeks. The guard protects you in the meantime.
botox
If severe
Ask about Botox (masseter injections)
Moderate evidence for reducing grinding force, effects 3–6 months. Worth discussing with your dentist if you have significant tooth wear or TMJ pain.
